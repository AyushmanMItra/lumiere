01 • THE HARD TRUTH

Web browsers were never built to be VLC.

Modern web browsers are engineered for the open web, not home theater archives. While engines like Chromium, WebKit, and Gecko natively support .mp4 and .webm containers, high-definition personal libraries almost universally rely on .mkv (Matroska) packaging high-bitrate video with multi-channel surround audio like Dolby Digital (AC-3, E-AC-3) or DTS. Because multi-channel surround codecs carry proprietary patent licensing pools, browser vendors legally refuse native decoders. Drag an .mkv file into any web browser, and it either fails to parse the container or plays video in total, eerie silence.

A common developer impulse is to bridge this gap client-side using in-browser WebAssembly (WASM) decoders like FFmpeg.wasm. In practice, software-decoding high-bitrate 1080p or 4K containers without hardware acceleration overwhelms laptop CPUs and mobile SoCs. It triggers aggressive thermal throttling, drains batteries in minutes, drops frames during high-action scenes, and crashes browser tabs as memory allocations collide with V8 heap limits.

Meanwhile, traditional cloud streaming workarounds require uploading 5 GB to 15 GB movie files to third-party servers. On typical residential uplink speeds (10–25 Mbps), uploading a single film wastes 30 to 60+ minutes before a watch party can even begin.

02 • THE COMPROMISE TRAP

Every existing workaround breaks the experience.

COMPROMISE

Screen Sharing

DISCORD & ZOOM

Real-time screen capture re-encodes the host's desktop under aggressive bitrate limits. Discord caps free streams at 720p and 30 fps (locking 1080p behind a $9.99/mo Nitro subscription). Dark scenes dissolve into heavy macroblocking, capturing 24fps film causes severe telecine judder and audio-video desync, and remote friends have zero personal autonomy over subtitle tracks or volume.

COMPROMISE

Local Sync Tools

SYNCPLAY & MPV-SYNC

Tools like Syncplay coordinate playhead timestamps between standalone desktop media players (VLC, mpv), but they do not stream video. Every friend must manually locate, download, and verify identical 5 GB to 15 GB files hours in advance. There is zero integrated voice or video chat, requiring users to juggle disparate third-party call apps.

COMPROMISE

Commercial Walled Gardens

TELEPARTY & SCENER

Traditional services lock you into paid catalogs with zero support for personal MKVs, indie cinema, or home rips. Lumière bridges both worlds: stream uncompressed local high-bitrate MKV files with zero disk wear, OR sync major streaming services (Netflix, Prime Video, JioHotstar, Zee5) with frame-accurate micro-steering alongside Lumière's integrated WebRTC voice, video, and cinema stage.

COMPROMISE

Dedicated Media Servers

PLEX & JELLYFIN

Designed for 24/7 home server infrastructure, demanding complex router port forwarding (port 32400 for Plex, 8096 for Jellyfin), dynamic DNS, reverse proxies, and SSL management. When multiple friends connect from varied devices, simultaneous GPU/CPU transcoding triggers heavy thermal load and fan noise, transforming movie night into a server administration chore.

03 • THE ARCHITECTURE

Host-side remuxing with zero-loss stream copy.

Lumière completely eliminates cloud uploads. The media file never leaves the host's machine, and friends stream directly on demand without waiting on multi-gigabyte downloads. For native web containers (.mp4 and .webm), the Express backend serves direct byte ranges via HTTP 206 Partial Content, delivering instantaneous timeline scrubbing with zero FFmpeg invocation.

When streaming high-definition .mkv files, Lumière runs an intelligent on-the-fly packaging pipeline. Instead of crushing video through costly GPU/CPU re-encoding, FFmpeg performs a zero-loss video stream copy (-c:v copy), extracting raw H.264 or HEVC bitstream packets and remuxing them on the fly into fragmented MP4 (fMP4) via -movflags frag_keyframe+empty_moov+default_base_moof. The video pipeline operates at near 0% CPU load, preserving 100% of the film's original color depth, bit depth, and crisp master clarity.

To conquer browser audio silence, Lumière selectively normalizes surround sound tracks. Only proprietary multi-channel codecs (such as 5.1 DTS or Dolby AC-3/E-AC-3) are downmixed into crystal-clear stereo AAC (-c:a aac -b:a 192k -ac 2). This targeted audio transcode consumes negligible host overhead (under 2–3% CPU) while delivering immediate, synchronized audio to every connected web client.

Crucially, the synchronization and media layers are decoupled. Playback synchronization is coordinated through sub-second Socket.IO heartbeats with automated rate steering (adjusting between 0.95× and 1.06× to eliminate buffer underruns without pitch distortion). Meanwhile, voice chat, webcams, and reactions operate on an isolated peer-to-peer WebRTC mesh (RTCPeerConnection). If someone's Wi-Fi drops voice packets, the movie stream never stutters, drops frames, or buffers.

04 • THE PHILOSOPHY

Self-hosted, private, and built for friends.

Lumière was built on a simple conviction: watching cinema with people you care about should not be an exercise in surveillance capitalism, subscription fees, or cloud gatekeeping. There are no account sign-ups, no passwords to manage, and no centralized databases harvesting user profiles. Room states are strictly ephemeral, living entirely in host memory and evaporating the instant a screening ends.

There are zero analytics trackers, third-party telemetry scripts, or cloud servers logging your watch history. You retain complete sovereignty over your bandwidth, your files, and your privacy.

Connecting is seamless and secure. Lumière operates directly over your local home Wi-Fi or across zero-configuration private mesh networks like Tailscale and WireGuard. By leveraging direct mesh routing, you get end-to-end encrypted cinema streaming between friends without ever opening router ports (no UPnP or public 32400/8096 forwarding required). It is private, resilient software designed purely for the joy of shared cinema.
